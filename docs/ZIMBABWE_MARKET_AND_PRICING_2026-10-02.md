# Zimbabwe market and pricing strategy for MyOffice

Research date: 2 October 2026. Currency: USD. Public prices can change.

## Decision

Build the recurring-revenue business around operational control for mines,
engineering contractors, workshops, manufacturers, equipment hire and fleets.
Qualify wholesalers and stores as the next adjacent segment. Handle the serious
driving-school and ecommerce prospects as narrowly scoped offers rather than
turning the mine application into a universal product for every industry.

The best prospect is an established organisation with recurring transactions,
valuable assets or stock, several people handing work between them, a manager
who wants visibility, and an event that makes change urgent. Sector alone does
not establish willingness to buy. Payment plus sustained operational use is
the validation; compliments, feature requests and demonstrations are not.

This is desk research, not a survey of prospective buyers or proof of paid
adoption. Rankings and recommended prices are hypotheses to test through paid
pilots. Vendor prices are advertised offers, not verified transaction prices,
customer counts or endorsements of the products. No national subscription
conversion rate or quantified national cost of disorganisation was found.

## Product boundary

The current product brief defines MyOffice as a mine-engineering ERP/MIS with
maintenance/work orders as a critical workflow. Its tools, custody, employee
eligibility, approvals, timesheets, overtime, leave and records create a useful
operational niche. This review did not certify production readiness.

Sell verified operational modules as an engineering operations MIS/CMMS. Do
not claim a full financial ERP, statutory payroll, core banking, complete POS,
restaurant platform or clinical system without those capabilities being built
and verified. Integrate with a customer's established accounting/ERP where
appropriate instead of demanding immediate replacement.

## How large is the underlying problem?

ZIMSTAT's preliminary census reports 204,798 operational establishments:
76.1% informal and 87.9% micro. It reports 21,396 small, 1,298 medium and
2,031 large establishments. These are establishments, not necessarily separate
companies, buying organisations or subscriptions. [ZIMSTAT census presentation](https://www.zimstat.co.zw/wp-content/uploads/production/EconomicCensus/EC2025_PRESENTATION.pdf)

Applying the rounded formal share gives approximately 48,947 formal
establishments: 204,798 x 23.9%. Applying the published size-specific formal
shares gives approximately 14,896 formal non-micro establishments:
21,396 x 54.7% + 1,298 x 89.5% + 2,031. These are rough screening frames,
not MyOffice's addressable market. Do not add them together: the latter is a
subset. Sector coverage, branch duplication, incumbents, access, budget and
workflow fit greatly reduce the reachable market. [Same census](https://www.zimstat.co.zw/wp-content/uploads/production/EconomicCensus/EC2025_PRESENTATION.pdf)

FinScope's 2022 MSME findings report 54% keeping financial records and 21%
having up-to-date financial/accounting records. Its resource table reports
78% with a cellphone and 2% with a laptop/computer. The 2022 population and
methodology differ from the establishment census; do not combine their totals
or interpret these as today's ERP demand. The evidence supports widespread
record gaps and a need for accessible mobile workflows, not a claim that every
business will buy software. [FinScope findings hosted by IPEC, PDF pages 31 and 65](https://ipec.co.zw/wp-content/uploads/findata/vfm-admin/vfm-downloader.php?h=2ff3583a5cce2c48e60f4036250a69a3&q=dXBsb2Fkcy9QdWJsaWNhdGlvbnMvUmVwb3J0cy9GaW5TY29wZS1NU01FLVN1cnZleS1aaW1iYWJ3ZS0yMDIyLVRvcGxpbmUtZmluZGluZ3MtdjFfMjNBdWcyMDIyX2NvbXByZXNzZWQucGRm)

There is no sound basis here to assign a national dollar value to lost records,
theft or downtime. Measure each buyer's actual consequences: overdue tools,
unresolved work orders, disputed overtime, unaccounted stock, unpaid invoices,
double bookings, repeated reconciliation and audit preparation. Good Excel
records can be adequate in a small operation. Replacement is justified when
handoffs, approvals, scale or auditability exceed the current workflow.

Do not dismiss non-buyers as lazy. Barriers can include limited cash flow,
no implementer, acceptable existing tools, fear of disruption, unclear value,
security concerns and a requirement for offline operation.

## Segment priorities and prices to test

The following are proposed scoped subscription ranges, not measured market
averages. Exclude applicable taxes, hardware, travel, messaging/payment fees,
special integrations and substantial custom development. Setup is separate.
Readiness thresholds are practical screening heuristics, not industry laws.

|---|---|---|---:|---|

| Churches | Administrator + authorised committee; paid staff, many branches/assets, donor reporting | Finance/admin records and asset control | 20–60 small; 75–200 established group | Only budgeted, owned projects |
| NGOs | Operations/finance head; donor asset tracking, audit/report deadlines | Assets, approvals, project time/evidence | 100–400; large donor scope quoted | Fit can be strong; funding/procurement risk |
| Banks/insurers/regulated lenders | Facilities/operations sponsor + procurement/IT/security | Non-core assets, maintenance and approvals | Quote after diligence; no credible generic figure | Later enterprise opportunity |
| Public institutions/utilities | Department owner + procurement; asset/compliance mandates | Narrow asset/maintenance workflow | Quote after procurement and support scope | Later; not initial cash-flow base |

Potential willingness is conditional in every row. An informal owner-led firm
can pay promptly; a large formal company can delay procurement or already have
a superior incumbent. Size and registration are filters, not guarantees.

## Pricing MyOffice for the mine

Recommended first quotes to test, assuming delivered and supported scope:

1. Tools and compliance department: $450/month, setup $750–1,500. Defined
   department/site, account cap and data-import allowance; existing tested
   custody, employee eligibility, inspections and gate-pass workflows only.
2. Engineering operations site: $900/month, setup $1,500–3,000. Defined work
   orders, tools, time/overtime/leave and reporting scope; phased rollout.
3. Broader supported site package: from $1,500/month, setup $3,000–6,000,
   only after scope, support, integration and acceptance requirements are known.

These are commercial hypotheses, not Dallaglio's approved budget. Price the
defined deliverable and support burden, not company revenue or the number of
features on a demo. Negotiate a limited paid pilot if buyer confidence is low.
Do not offer the entire mine platform with unlimited development for $50.

Padenga publicly describes Dallaglio's commercial gold operations and its
Eureka/Pickstone mines. Its sustainability page reports ISO 9001, 14001 and
45001 certification at the mines in December 2024/January 2025. This is an
evidence-based reason to discuss traceable operational records and controls,
not proof that the company needs this particular system or will purchase it.
[Operations](https://www.padenga.com/mining), [sustainability](https://www.padenga.com/mining-sustainability).

Confirm the actual contracting entity with procurement rather than assuming
the Dallaglio/Breckridge relationship or name on an invoice. If development was
done during employment or using an employer's assets/data, clarify software
ownership and commercial reuse permission before selling it elsewhere. Never
reuse mine data, internal SOPs or branding without permission.

## Pricing economics and ROI

Price floor per month = hosting/backups + support time x internal hourly cost
+ allocated maintenance/security cost + amortised acquisition/onboarding
+ overhead + margin. Setup must cover implementation effort; it is not MRR.

Illustration, not measured cost: at $50/month, $8 allocated infrastructure and
2 support hours at $15/hour leave $12 before development, selling, payment
fees and overhead. A fourth support hour makes that customer loss-making.
Small plans require standard templates and strict support/configuration scope.

For value validation, record a baseline and show improvement. Example only:
60 hours/month genuinely released at $5/hour = $300; separately verified
avoidable tool losses reduced by $250; attributable stock losses reduced by
$400: $950 benefit. At $150/month, net benefit is $800; net benefit/fee is
5.33. A $600 setup would have a 0.75-month simple payback on those assumptions.
Released salaried time is capacity, not necessarily cash saved. Do not count
the same loss twice or count all sales recovered as profit. Downtime benefits
need defensible contribution-margin and attribution calculations. Replace
every example input with the customer's records before using it in a quote.

## Diagnose the actual warm leads

- Other mine: strong product fit if a sponsor, budget and implementer exist.
  Ask to review recent tools/work-order/overtime reconciliation, then propose
  a paid one-department pilot. Do not infer sales readiness from the mine name.
- Stores automation prospect: ask whether this is consumable stores, wholesale,
  retail or production inventory. Uploading items alone is not the recurring
  benefit; investigate issues/receipts, reconciliation, reordering and losses.
- Driving school: prioritise a real operating owner with several cars or
  instructors. Ask for sample lesson/payment records and a booking conflict.
  Test a $49 simple or $99 broader monthly proposal plus $150–500 setup.
  Name the daily receptionist/operator and an actual implementation date.
- Serious INUKA seller: verify existing paid orders, stock, customer acquisition,
  margin and delivery capacity. Test $35–60/month plus $200–600 setup for a
  limited catalogue/order offer. Higher management work costs extra. A website
  does not create customers by itself. Check current reseller/brand policies,
  image rights and gateway onboarding before launch. [INUKA terms](https://inuka.co.za/terms-of-use/).
- Farmer: an estate with employees, machinery, inputs and buyer reporting is
  very different from an occasional/small producer. Ask what must be reconciled
  every week and who does it; if there is no recurring operator, defer.
- Three churches: identify the authorised budget owner, paid administrator,
  recurring process and decision meeting. A interested member's request is not
  an institutional purchasing decision. Consider committee-approved annual or
  quarterly billing when appropriate; no speculative custom work beforehand.
- Teacher: ask whether paid tutoring students already exist. $50/month is
  $600/year, which may be unjustified for unvalidated lesson sales. A simple
  landing page or established learning platform can be sufficient. A school
  bursar/proprietor with fee collection needs is a different buyer. Place this
  lead in nurture until content, customers and a payment commitment exist.
- Interested but inactive ecommerce lead: send one clear scoped offer and
  next-step date. If there is no response/action, archive for later follow-up.

None of these explanations establishes why an individual did not buy; discover
whether the barrier was budget, value, authority, timing, trust or workload.

## Qualification: replace interest with observable commitment

Ask these in a short discovery meeting, before building a tailored demo:

1. Show how this process worked last week. Which records and handoffs exist?
2. What went wrong recently? What did it cost in money, time or delayed work?
3. How often does that happen and which transactions are affected?
4. Who owns the problem, approves spending and can sign the purchase?
5. What are you already paying for software/accounting/control of this work?
6. Why act now? Audit, expansion, loss event, new manager or reporting deadline?
7. Who will run the system daily and import/verify the opening records?
8. Which existing software, devices and connectivity must it work with?
9. If we prove the agreed outcome, can you approve this fee and setup scope?
10. Can we agree the pilot, operator, records needed and decision date now?

Score 0–2 each for measurable pain, urgency, authority, budget and rollout
ownership. This is an internal sales heuristic, not a researched prediction.
8–10: paid pilot proposal. 5–7: close the specific missing condition. 0–4:
nurture/archive. A clear zero in purchasing authority, viable budget or daily
operator prevents starting customised implementation regardless of total.

Distinguish stages: interested -> qualified -> funded pilot -> live use -> paid
renewal. For a corporate buyer, budget confirmation and an accepted purchase
order/milestone contract can show commitment even when deposits are prohibited.
Follow the procurement process; do not label every slow enterprise unserious.
For an owner-led SME, an actual setup deposit is a stronger signal than words.
Any sample-record transfer must be authorised and minimise personal data.

## Paid pilot and renewal discipline

Use one department/workflow and a 30–45 day pilot (longer where the cycle
requires it). Define baseline, acceptance criteria, named operator, training,
customer data responsibilities, support limits, fee and conversion date.
Suggested test prices: SME pilot $100–250; industrial pilot $300–750 depending
on effort. Credit some pilot cost to setup if agreed, not automatically.
Do not promise measured financial savings when controls lack a valid baseline.

Pilot criteria examples: opening register reconciles; representative users can
complete the agreed workflow; approvals/audit/export reconcile; weekly review
occurs; target share of eligible movements is captured; failures are handled
truthfully. Select numeric targets jointly rather than declaring an arbitrary
universal percentage. Limited authorised test transactions are planned, never
casual mutations of production records.

Track eligible-workflow coverage, time to first useful outcome, active operator
use, support hours, invoice collection, renewal and attributable control/value
improvement. Signups/page views alone do not measure adoption.

Invoice recurring service in advance where agreed; offer quarterly payment or
term-aligned school billing when it suits cash flow. Provide several reliable
payment methods and formal invoices. State currency, taxes, late-payment and
data-export rules clearly; agree any lawful alternate-currency settlement
method before invoicing. Do not assume everyone has recurring card billing.
Quote marketing, messaging, payment fees, hardware and travel separately.
Discount for a narrower scope, limited paid pilot or annual prepayment only
when the economics remain healthy. No unlimited custom features/support.

## Local commercial and technical requirements

- Fiscalisation: a sales/POS product must assess current ZIMRA requirements.
  Official guidance offers hardware/approved-supplier onboarding and a direct
  virtual API development route with testing/registration. It is incorrect to
  repeat vendor claims that direct developer integration is universally
  impossible. Partnership may still be commercially easier. Do not market
  fiscal compliance until the applicable workflow is accepted and tested.
  [ZIMRA FDMS](https://www.zimra.co.zw/news/22-taxmans-corner/2307-compliance-with-the-zimra-fiscalisation-data-management-system-fdms).
- Privacy: clarify controller/processor responsibilities, relevant licensing,
  security, retention, incident handling and cross-border cloud arrangements.
  Schools, churches, clinics and workforce records warrant particular care.
  [POTRAZ implementation guidance hosted by Veritas](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20and%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Implementation%20Guidlines.pdf).
- Banks: substantial vendor/security/continuity requirements make this a later
  market. Begin with non-core facilities/assets if invited, not deposits,
  lending, payments or core banking. [RBZ August 2025 guideline](https://www.rbz.co.zw/documents/Regulations_Acts/2025/Cybersecurity_and_Resilience_Guideline_-_August_2025.pdf).
- Ecommerce: confirm gateway availability, fees, merchant approval, refunds,
  settlement and end-to-end integration. Paynow offers local merchant services;
  that does not certify a particular Shopify/plugin configuration.
  [Paynow business](https://www.paynow.co.zw/home/businesshome).
- Reliability: mobile-friendly, low-bandwidth workflows, predictable backups,
  restore tests, export, permission isolation and competent support are part of
  the sold service. A retail checkout needs offline resilience/hardware that
  a cloud-only records application cannot simply assume. Do not promise an
  SLA that free-tier infrastructure or current operations cannot support.
- Multiple clients: independently validate tenant isolation, provisioning,
  backups, upgrades and support boundaries before onboarding unrelated firms.
  A department filter within one mine is not proof of customer data isolation.

These are buying/implementation constraints, not a legal compliance certificate.

## Finding prospects and building a reachable market

Start where you have access: existing mine relationships, authorised supplier
and contractor introductions, accountants/bookkeepers, stocktake firms and
equipment service providers. Offer value to referrers; referrals still require
qualification. Obtain permission before using customer names, logos or results.

The [Chamber of Mines](https://www.chamines.co.zw/) includes mines, equipment,
spares and chemicals suppliers plus service providers. The
[SMEAZ directory](https://www.smeaz.org.zw/index.php/component/jbusinessdirectory/catalog)
contains operating businesses to screen. ZimTrade's
[2025 trade directory](https://tradezimbabwe.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/TradeDirectory_202501_web-1.pdf)
is a candidate source for exporters/processors; its PDF failed to open in this
research, so individual entries were not verified. These are prospecting
sources, never lists of confirmed buyers. Prioritise nearby firms to control
on-site implementation costs, then expand through proven referrals.

For schools, approach proprietors/bursars and approved decision structures,
not individual interested teachers. For stores, approach the owner and person
responsible for reconciliation. For mines, include finance/IT/procurement as
well as engineering. User enthusiasm alone does not authorise a purchase.

## Ninety-day plan and revenue scenarios

Days 1–15: agree ownership/commercial permissions, verify one dependable
operational package, prepare a real demo using authorised/anonymised records,
define setup/support boundaries and interview warm leads. Build a shortlist
of 30 named operational firms; this is a target, not a measured market size.

Days 16–45: run roughly 12 qualified discovery conversations, seek 3 funded
pilots, review outcomes weekly. Numbers are planning targets, not forecast
conversion rates. Pursue the driving-school and serious seller only with a
standardised narrow scope and their payment commitment. Do not start several
unfunded vertical products simultaneously.

Days 46–90: convert accepted pilots, document authorised evidence, obtain
referrals, and repeat the same offer. If no one funds a pilot, re-examine
segment, urgency, trust, scope and offer before building more features.

Illustrative monthly revenue arithmetic, not a forecast:

| Scenario | MRR | Annualised recurring run rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5 operational customers x $400 | $2,000 | $24,000 |
| 3 industrial customers x $650 + 10 small customers x $75 | $2,700 | $32,400 |
| 50 small customers x $50 | $2,500 | $30,000 |

Annualised run rate assumes that current subscriptions continue; it is not
contracted/collected revenue. Deduct churn, bad debt, support, infrastructure,
sales costs, taxes and development to estimate actual profit. Larger contracts
bring procurement time, concentration and heavier support; fifty small accounts
bring greater billing/onboarding load. Compare contribution per support hour,
not subscription revenue alone.

My recommendation is an industrial anchor business with a small number of
repeatable adjacent SME packages, not a catalogue of bespoke apps funded by
optimistic expressions of interest.
